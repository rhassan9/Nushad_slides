## What is Conflict?

> *"When two or more social entities (individuals, groups, organisations, nations) come into contact with one another, their relationships may become inconsistent or incompatible."*  
> — Rahim (2001)

> *"Conflict is a process that begins when one party perceives that another party has negatively affected, or is about to negatively affect, something that the first party cares about."*  
> — Buchanan & Huczynski (2023)

Key characteristics:
- Requires **both a thinking and a feeling element**
- Must be **perceived by both parties** — if either is unaware, no conflict exists
- Triggers **reactive behaviours** directed at the other party
- Can arise from: different attitudes, behaviours, values, scarce resources, or power/status struggles

> ⚠️ *Left to fester, disagreements can build into unhealthy conflict that damages relationships and leads to wider discord.* — CIPD (2025)

## Causes of Conflict

### Level of Conflict
| Level | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dyad** | Between two individuals | Manager vs. subordinate; two peers |
| **Group** | Within a work group or team | Face-to-face, virtual, or hybrid teams |
| **Organisation** | From how the company is structured | Inter-departmental conflict |

### Type of Conflict (Buchanan & Huczynski, 2023)
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Relationship** | Conflict over interpersonal issues, e.g. personality clash |
| **Task** | Conflict over goals and content of work, e.g. how best to solve a problem |
| **Process** | Conflict over how a task is accomplished, e.g. allocation of tasks |

---

## Effects of Conflict

### Positive (Functional) Effects — Rahim (2001)
1. Greater innovation, creativity & growth
2. Improved decision-making
3. Different solutions to a problem
4. Enhanced individual and group performance
5. Clarification of positions

### Negative (Dysfunctional) Effects — Rahim (2001)
1. Job stress, burnout and dissatisfaction
2. Impaired communication between parties
3. A climate of distrust and suspicion
4. Damaged relationships
5. Reduced levels of job performance
6. Reduced levels of commitment and loyalty

## Managing Conflict

### Approaches to Managing Conflict (Thomas-Kilmann Model)
Measured across two dimensions: **Assertiveness** (satisfying your own concerns) and **Cooperativeness** (satisfying others' concerns).

| Approach | Assertiveness | Cooperativeness | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Competing** | High | Low | Win-Lose |
| **Avoiding** | Low | Low | Lose-Lose |
| **Accommodating** | Low | High | Lose-Win |
| **Compromising** | Medium | Medium | Partial Win-Win |
| **Collaborating** | High | High | Win-Win |

---

### Mediation

Mediation is an **informal, voluntary, and confidential** process where an impartial third party helps those in dispute reach a mutually acceptable resolution.

**Mediation may be unsuitable when:** (CIPD, 2025)
- A decision about right or wrong is needed (e.g. criminal activity or overt abuse)
- An individual bringing a discrimination or harassment case wants it formally investigated, or the allegation is of a serious nature
- An individual has mental health problems or learning difficulties that would obstruct a joint meeting
- The parties don't have the authority to settle the issue

---

### CIPD (2020) Report: "Managing Conflict in the Modern Workplace"
Based on a survey of **1,016 senior HR professionals and decision-makers** in the UK.

Key findings included insight into the prevalence of workplace conflict, the most common types (interpersonal and task-based), and the effectiveness of mediation vs. formal procedures.

### Management Skills for Resolving Conflict (Institute of Directors, 2023)

**9 Key Skills:**

| Skill | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Active Listening** | Enables understanding and empathy; helps determine the true source of conflict |
| **2. Problem-solving & Flexibility** | Discovers best compromise; prevents recurrence through innovative methods |
| **3. Communication** | Facilitates clear expectations; enables questions and expressions of opinion |
| **4. Collaboration** | Builds a team culture of accountability and trust; promotes good morale |
| **5. Stress Management** | Managing your own stress to stay positive; prevents misunderstandings from personal frustration |
| **6. Positive Attitude** | Using humour (when appropriate); treating others with integrity and respect |
| **7. Emotional Intelligence** | Developing solutions through empathy; recognising colleagues' motivations and emotions |
| **8. Neutrality & Impartiality** | Encouraging all perspectives; generating a compromise that benefits everyone |
| **9. Patience** | Remaining calm when others have heightened emotions; allowing time and space to de-escalate |
