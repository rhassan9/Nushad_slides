# Lecture: Managing Conflict in Teams

## What is Conflict?
Conflict is an inevitable part of human interaction. It occurs when two or more social entities (individuals, groups, organizations) come into contact and their relationships become inconsistent or incompatible.
* **Causes:** Different attitudes, behaviors, values, struggles for scarce resources, or competition for power and status.
* **Definition:** Conflict is a process that begins when one party perceives that another party has negatively affected, or is about to negatively affect, something the first party cares about. It requires both a thinking and a feeling element, triggering reactive behaviors.

*If left to fester, disagreements can build into unhealthy conflict that damages relationships.*

## Causes of Conflict
Conflict in the workplace can occur at three levels:
1. **Dyad:** Between two individuals (e.g., manager and subordinate).
2. **Group:** Within a workgroup or team (intra-group).
3. **Organization:** Arising from how the company is structured (inter-departmental).

The causes generally fall into three broad headings:
1. **Relationship:** Interpersonal issues and personality clashes.
2. **Task:** Disagreements over the goals and content of the work.
3. **Process:** Disagreements over how a task is accomplished (e.g., role allocation).

## Effects of Conflict
Conflict is not always inherently bad; it can have both positive and negative effects depending on how it is managed.

### Positive (Functional) Effects
* Leads to greater innovation, creativity, and growth.
* Improves decision-making processes by challenging the status quo.
* Enhances the quality of group work by encouraging diverse viewpoints.

### Negative (Dysfunctional) Effects
* Slows down or halts communication, leading to project delays.
* Reduces team cohesiveness and causes relationships to deteriorate.
* In extreme cases, can lead to the destruction of the team.

## Managing Conflict
Conflict management involves strategies to minimize the negative effects and maximize the positive aspects of conflict.

### Approaches to Managing Conflict (Thomas-Kilmann Model)
People generally have a preferred style of handling conflict, measured across two dimensions: *Assertiveness* (satisfying one's own concerns) and *Cooperativeness* (satisfying the other party's concerns).
1. **Competing (Assertive & Uncooperative):** Pursuing one's own concerns at the other person's expense (win-lose).
2. **Avoiding (Unassertive & Uncooperative):** Not addressing the conflict at all; withdrawing from the situation (lose-lose).
3. **Accommodating (Unassertive & Cooperative):** Neglecting one's own concerns to satisfy the other person; yielding to another's point of view (lose-win).
4. **Compromising (Moderate Assertiveness & Cooperativeness):** Finding an expedient, mutually acceptable solution that partially satisfies both parties.
5. **Collaborating (Assertive & Cooperative):** Working together to find a solution that fully satisfies the concerns of both parties (win-win).

### Resolving Conflict: Mediation
When conflicts cannot be resolved internally, mediation may be necessary. It is an informal, voluntary, and confidential process where an impartial third party helps those in dispute reach a mutually acceptable agreement.

### Key Management Skills for Resolving Conflict (Institute of Directors, 2023)
* **Active Listening:** Understanding true sources of conflict through empathy.
* **Problem-solving and Flexibility:** Finding innovative compromises.
* **Communication:** Facilitating clear expectations.
* **Collaboration:** Developing a team culture founded on trust.
* **Stress Management:** Managing personal frustration to maintain positivity.
* **Positive Attitude:** Using humor (when appropriate) and acting with integrity.
* **Emotional Intelligence:** Recognizing colleagues' motivations and emotions.
* **Neutrality and Impartiality:** Treating all perspectives fairly.
* **Patience:** Allowing space for heightened emotions to calm down.
