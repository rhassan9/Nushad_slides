## Managing People in the Workplace

> *"People management forms a large part of every manager's job, whether they work in a large multinational organisation, a not-for-profit organisation or a small charity. Organisations also increasingly aim for all employees to be motivated and involved, so an understanding of the subject is important for everyone."*  
> — Hook & Jenkins (2019)

---

## What is HRM?

### Definition 1 — Baum (2006)
HRM consists of a **three-part cycle**:

| Part | Activities |
|---|---|
| **1. Attracting an Effective Workforce** | Labour markets, recruitment and selection, HR planning, flexible employment patterns, retention |
| **2. Development** | Performance appraisal, education, training and development |
| **3. Maintaining an Effective Workforce** | Rewards, welfare, teamwork, empowerment, employment relations, grievance and discipline, equality and diversity |

### Definition 2 — Torrington et al. (2020)
> HRM is frequently used to refer to the **range of activities involved in managing the relationship an organisation has with its people** — employees and others hired under various contractual arrangements.

## Six Purposes of HR Activity (Torrington et al., 2020)

| Purpose | Description |
|---|---|
| **1. Staffing** | Attracting and retaining the right people |
| **2. Performance** | Managing and improving individual and team performance |
| **3. Change-management** | Supporting organisational change and transitions |
| **4. Administration** | Maintaining accurate HR records and compliance |
| **5. Reputation** | Building the organisation's employer brand |
| **6. Well-being** | Supporting the physical and mental health of employees |

## Managing Performance in the Workplace

> *"Performance management is a process which contributes to the effective management of individuals and teams in order to achieve high levels of organisational performance. As such, it establishes shared understanding about what is to be achieved and an approach to leading and developing people which will ensure it is achieved."*  
> — Armstrong and Baron (2004)

**Why it matters (CIPD, 2026):**
- People are the **greatest source of organisational value**
- Employees must understand what's expected of them
- Must be given: motivation, skills, resources, and support
- Must be held **accountable** for their work

---

## Tools for Performance Management (CIPD, 2014)

Six main tools:

### 1. Performance Appraisal (CIPD, 2026)
Performance reviews typically serve **two purposes**:
1. To inform **administrative decisions** — pay, promotion, redundancies
2. To support **learning and development**

> ⚠️ *Research shows these two purposes involve different cognitive processes — it is best to focus on them separately, not try to do both in one meeting.*

Each stage of the appraisal process should have a clear, defined purpose.

---

### 2. 360-Degree Feedback (Hook & Jenkins, 2019)
Feedback is gathered from **multiple sources** — not just the line manager:
- The employee themselves (self-appraisal)
- Colleagues within the same team
- Subordinates who work for that person
- The manager(s) to whom they report

> The appraisee is placed at the **centre** of the feedback process.

---

### 3. Learning and Talent Management (CIPD, 2025)

Organisations take different approaches to talent management:

| Approach | Description |
|---|---|
| **Exclusive** | Focus on high-performing or high-potential individuals only |
| **Inclusive** | Broader approach covering all employees; everyone has strengths to develop |
| **Blended** | Develops all employees while prioritising a core group |

> The organisation must determine how it manages the performance of its talent — there is no single right answer.

---

### 4. Objectives & Performance Standards (Hook & Jenkins, 2019)

Two approaches to assessing performance:

| Approach | Description |
|---|---|
| **Output-based** | Uses objectives and sets targets for the employee to work towards |
| **Input-based** | Examines the level of competence the employee must achieve in their role |

For the **output/objectives approach** to work effectively:
- The organisation must be clear about its **strategic direction**
- Individual and team objectives must **contribute** to the overall strategy
- Objectives must be **SMART** (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)

---

### 5. Measurement — 6 Steps (Indeed, 2026)
Steps to measure employee performance effectively:
1. Prepare feedback in advance
2. Give employees time to prepare
3. Provide documentation
4. Make the meeting a **conversation**
5. End with a **positive outlook** of the employee's future
6. Evaluate performance and contributions

---

### 6. Performance Related Pay (PRP) (CIPD, 2026)

Linking pay to individual, team, and organisational performance remains a common approach.

**Arguments for PRP:**
- Pay is a strong motivator — not just a 'hygiene factor' as Herzberg often implied
- Keeps performance management central to pay reviews

**Arguments against / risks of PRP:**
- Can **demotivate** employees
- Can **incentivise undesirable behaviour** if targets are set too narrowly
- May not fully reflect what constitutes good performance

> Steps can be taken to avoid unintended consequences and make PRP feel **fairer**.

---

## Good Performance Management — Principles (CIPD, 2026)

- There is **no single best approach** — it must fit the organisation's specific context
- Performance management should be a **continuous cycle**, not an isolated event
- Employee objectives should **evolve** alongside changing business priorities
- Feedback should be **regular, timely, and focused on improvement**
- Teams and functions should be able to manage performance in a way relevant to **their specific roles**

**Manager skills required for effective performance management (CIPD, 2025):**
- Communication
- Listening
- Feedback
- Coaching
- Emotional Intelligence

---

## Performance Review (CIPD, 2026)

Performance reviews are an important element within the broader performance management system.

**Purposes:**
- Inform administrative decisions (pay, bonuses, promotions, redundancy)
- Support professional growth
- Help shape objective setting for the **next performance cycle**
