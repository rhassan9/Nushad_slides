# Lecture 1: Module Introduction, Overview, and Team Formation

## Introduction to the Module
The "Teams, People and Performance" (MAN-10081) module aims to provide an understanding of the principles and practices that underpin effective teamwork. It explores roles, dynamics, and leadership impacts on collective outcomes. The focus is on applying theories to real-world scenarios to develop collaborative skills necessary in diverse organizational settings.

The module is led by Dr. Swati Garg and supported by Dr. Scott Bambrick, Director of Keele Business School.

## Assessment Overview
The primary assessment is a deep reflection on team-based exercises, requiring either a 5-10 minute podcast or a 1500-word written report. The submission must address three main areas:
* **Individual Role:** Your specific role, contributions, challenges faced, and lessons learned.
* **Team Dynamics:** How the group functioned, including communication methods, leadership styles, and strategies used.
* **Personal Development:** Skills gained and areas for future development in collaboration.

## Introduction to Teams and Group Dynamics
A team is defined as a small number of people with complementary skills committed to a common purpose and performance goals, holding themselves mutually accountable (Katzenbach & Smith, 1993). Essentially, a team is a group combined with a specific goal and a plan to reach it.

### Team Life Cycle Model (Tuckman & Jensen, 1977)
Teams generally pass through five stages of development:
* **Forming:** Members are recruited or allocated, establishing initial contact.
* **Storming:** Time to work out positions, often involving conflict or personality clashes.
* **Norming:** Setting rules (formal/informal) and establishing methods of working and roles.
* **Performing:** Executing the tasks with a focused, task-centered approach.
* **Adjourning/Mourning:** The conclusion of the project and breaking up of the team.

## Team Processes and Management
Effective team management requires setting clear objectives, timescales, and standards. Management styles can vary from prescriptive and authoritarian to self-directed and collaborative.

* **Team Formation:** Involves balancing qualities like experience, ability, and personality.
* **Processes and Rules:** Teams benefit from setting 'rules of engagement' or a team charter that outlines the purpose, responsibilities, and workloads.
* **Management Control:** Requires allocating project work, providing direct supervision, or acting as a mentor/facilitator depending on the task's nature.
* **Problematic Members:** Issues should be addressed by obtaining information, discussing formally or informally, and monitoring behavior. If necessary, project involvement or roles can be adjusted.

## Belbin's Team Roles
Members typically have dual roles: their functional job role and their team role. Belbin identifies nine team roles based on intelligence, dominance, extroversion, and stability. A well-functioning team usually requires a mix of these roles, balancing extroverts/introverts and creative/analytical thinkers.

* **Plant:** Creative and unorthodox problem solver, but can ignore details.
* **Resource Investigator:** Enthusiastic and communicative, explores opportunities, but may lose interest quickly.
* **Co-ordinator:** Confident and clarifies goals, but can be seen as manipulative.
* **Shaper:** Dynamic and thrives on pressure, but can provoke others.
* **Monitor Evaluator:** Strategic and discerning, judges accurately, but may lack the ability to inspire.
* **Team Worker:** Co-operative and perceptive, calms waters, but can be indecisive.
* **Implementer:** Reliable and practical, turns ideas into action, but can be inflexible.
* **Completer/Finisher:** Conscientious and anxious, searches out errors, but reluctant to delegate.
* **Specialist:** Single-minded and provides rare skills, but dwells on technicalities.

## Practical Advice for Students
Every team task involves two challenges: completing the task itself and managing the team dynamics. A good manager dedicates significant effort to keeping the team together.

Helpful hints for group work include:
* Assigning someone to take notes and distribute them.
* Setting clear ground rules and meeting times.
* Establishing individual targets to maintain accountability.
