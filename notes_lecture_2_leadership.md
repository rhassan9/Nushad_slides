## What is Leadership?

There is no single agreed-upon definition of leadership:

> *"Leadership is a set of behaviors used to help people align their collective direction, to execute strategic plans, and to continually renew an organization."*  
> — McKinsey & Company (2022)

> *"Leadership is a process of social influence, which maximizes the efforts of others, towards the achievement of a goal."*  
> — Forbes / Kevin Kruse (2013)

> *"Leadership is about empowering others to achieve things they did not think possible… pointing in the direction, articulating a vision of the world that does not yet exist, then asking help from others to make that vision happen."*  
> — Simon Sinek

> *"Leadership may be considered as the process (act) of influencing the activities of an organized group in its efforts toward goal setting and goal achievement."*  
> — Stogdill (1950)

> *"A genuine leader is not a searcher for consensus but a molder of consensus."*  
> — Martin Luther King Jr.

## Leaders vs. Managers

The terms "management" and "leadership" are often used interchangeably, but they differ in focus:

| **Leader** | **Manager** |
|---|---|
| Visionary | Rational |
| Passionate | Consulting |
| Creative | Persistent |
| Flexible | Problem solving |
| Inspiring | Tough minded |
| Innovative | Analytical |
| Courageous | Structured |
| Imaginative | Deliberate |
| Experimental | Authoritative |
| Independent | Stabilising |
| Shares knowledge | Centralises knowledge |

> *"A manager does the right thing whereas a leader does what is right."*

Leadership is about **coping with change**; management is about **coping with complexity**.  
- Strong leadership without management → chaos  
- Strong management without leadership → stagnation

- *"Anyone can become a leader. It's just a skill and can be taught like any other."*

## Types of Leadership Styles

| Style | Description |
|---|---|
| **Charismatic** | Uses charm, magnetism, and emotional appeal to inspire followers |
| **Traditional** | Power bestowed based on past traditions |
| **Situational** | Adapts leadership style based on who is being led |
| **Appointed** | Selected/designated to fill a formal role |
| **Functional** | Acts as part of the team's function |

## Leadership Theories

### 1. Trait Theory
Early theories suggested leaders are **born, not made**, focusing on inherent personal qualities.

Key traits identified by Daft (2016) for effective leaders:
- Self-confidence
- Honesty and Integrity
- Optimism
- Desire to Lead
- Independence and Interpersonal Skills

> *"Since Stogdill's early review, trait theory has made a come back. Recent research has made it clear that successful leaders are not like other people."*  
> — Kirkpatrick & Locke (1991)

**The Dark Side of the Big 5 Traits** (Judge et al., 2009):
- **Conscientious** leaders may avoid innovation and delay decisions
- **Extraverted** leaders may provide unclear strategic focus
- **Agreeable** leaders may minimise conflict at the cost of effectiveness
- **Emotionally stable** leaders may appear apathetic
- **Open** leaders may alienate followers needing direct instructions

---

### 2. Behavioural Theories
Shifted focus from what leaders *are* to what they *do* — suggesting leadership **can be taught**.

- **Ohio State Studies:** Two dimensions: *Initiating Structure* (task-focused) and *Consideration* (relationship-focused)
- **University of Michigan Studies:** *Employee-Oriented* vs. *Production-Oriented* leaders

---

### 3. Contingency Theories
Effectiveness of a leadership style **depends on the situation**.

- **Fiedler's Model:** Match between leader's style and degree of situational control
- **Hersey & Blanchard's Situational Leadership:** Adjust style based on follower "readiness" (ability + willingness)

**Hersey & Blanchard (1982)** rests on two assumptions:
1. There is no single all-purpose leadership style
2. Leader behaviour has two independent components: **directive** and **supportive** behaviour

The four styles:
| Quadrant | Style | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Directing** | Low competence, high commitment |
| S2 | **Coaching** | Some competence, low commitment |
| S3 | **Supporting** | High competence, variable commitment |
| S4 | **Delegating** | High competence, high commitment |

---

### 4. Transactional Leadership (TAL)
> *"Transactional leaders emphasise hard levers of change such as designing systems and controls."* — Johnson et al. (2017)

- Emphasises structures, targets, financial incentives, and performance monitoring
- *"Transactional leaders trade money, jobs and security for compliance."* — Armstrong (2012)
- Most managers use some form of TAL for results-oriented tasks (Tavanti, 2008)
- **Criticism:** Unclear underlying mechanism; little empirical evidence on organisational effects (Yukl, 1999)

---

### 5. Transformational Leadership (TFL)
> *"Transformational leaders emphasise building a vision for the organisation, creating an organisational identity around collective values and beliefs to support that vision and energising people to achieve it."* — Johnson et al. (2017)

Four components (Currie & Lockett, 2007):
- **Charisma**
- **Inspiration**
- **Individualised Consideration**
- **Intellectual Stimulation**

**Criticism:** van Knippenberg (2013) considers the concept "seriously flawed"; Alvesson & Karreman (2015) say key elements are arbitrary.

---

### 6. Servant Leadership
Coined by Robert K. Greenleaf (1970) — servant leaders put the **needs of others first**.

Ten characteristics (Spears, 1995 in Qiu & Dooley, 2018):
1. Listening
2. Empathy
3. Healing
4. Awareness
5. Persuasion
6. Conceptualisation
7. Foresight
8. Stewardship
9. Commitment to the growth of people
10. Building community

Key principles shared from personal experience:

- Leadership has **nothing to do with position or title**
- **Kindness:** Treat people how you would like to be treated
- **Honesty:** Always be honest; do not avoid difficult conversations

## Trust in Organizations

> *"Trust is a psychological state where one agrees to be vulnerable to another based on positive expectations."*

Trust is critical for effective leadership and team performance.

### Types of Trust
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Deterrence-based** | Based on fear of reprisal if trust is violated |
| **Knowledge-based** | Based on behavioral predictability from past interaction |
| **Identification-based** | Based on mutual understanding of intentions and values |

### Dimensions of Trust
- **Integrity** – Honesty and truthfulness
- **Competence** – Technical and interpersonal skills
- **Consistency** – Reliability and predictability
- **Loyalty** – Willingness to protect another person
- **Openness** – Providing the full truth

### Impact of Trust vs. Mistrust

**Benefits of Trust:**
- Increases retention
- Builds support for goals
- Enhances productivity
- Improves communication
- Speeds up decision-making
- Promotes cooperation

**Consequences of Mistrust:**
- Groups separate and pursue personal interests
- People conceal information and avoid collaboration
- Significant reduction in overall productivity

### Building Trust (Kramer's 7 Rules)
1. **Know yourself** – Understand your own trust tendencies
2. **Start small** – Build trust through minor, low-risk acts
3. **Write an escape clause** – Have a disengagement plan to reduce anxiety
4. **Send strong signals** – Clearly demonstrate trustworthiness and penalise abuse
5. **Recognise the other person's dilemma** – Understand their concerns about trusting you
6. **Look at roles** – Consider positional guarantees of expertise
7. **Remain vigilant** – Continuously update your assessment of trustworthiness
