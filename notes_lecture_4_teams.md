# Lecture 4: Teams, Leadership & Managing People

## Overview and Core Distinctions
This lecture connects three core lenses: working in groups and teams, understanding leadership in organizations, and managing people effectively (including motivation and well-being).

### Groups vs. Teams
While often used interchangeably, there are practical distinctions between groups and teams in a workplace context.
* **Group:** Individuals coordinate their efforts, but accountability is mainly individual. Goals are often assigned by external management, and the focus is on efficiency and clear boundaries.
* **Team:** Individuals are highly interdependent with shared accountability. They develop common goals and purposes internally, focusing on innovation, complex problem-solving, and shared leadership.

## Team Development and Effectiveness
The structure and evolution of a team deeply impact its success.

### Team Effectiveness Model
Effective teams require a balance of context, composition, and process.
* **Context:** Adequate resources, leadership, climate of trust, and performance evaluation/rewards.
* **Composition:** Abilities of members, personality (e.g., conscientiousness, openness), allocation of roles (like Belbin's roles), diversity, size, and flexibility.
* **Process:** Common purpose, specific goals, team efficacy, managed conflict levels, and minimized social loafing (individuals putting in less effort when working in a group).

### Social Loafing
This occurs when individuals reduce their effort when working in a group compared to working alone.
* **Causes:** Lack of individual accountability, perception that others aren't doing their fair share, or feeling that individual contributions don't matter.
* **Prevention:** Set clear group goals, increase inter-group competition, utilize peer evaluation, select highly motivated members, and base rewards partially on individual contributions.

## Managing People: Motivation and Job Design
Effective management involves designing jobs that motivate employees and evaluating performance fairly.

### Job Characteristics Model (Hackman & Oldham)
This model proposes that any job can be described in terms of five core dimensions that impact intrinsic motivation:
1. **Skill Variety:** The degree to which a job requires diverse activities and skills.
2. **Task Identity:** The degree to which a job requires completion of a whole and identifiable piece of work.
3. **Task Significance:** The impact the job has on others' lives or work.
4. **Autonomy:** The freedom and independence provided in scheduling and determining procedures.
5. **Feedback:** The direct and clear information received about performance effectiveness.

*High levels of these characteristics lead to critical psychological states (meaningfulness, responsibility, knowledge of results), which in turn lead to high motivation, performance, and satisfaction.*

### Performance Management
Managing performance involves more than just annual reviews. It includes establishing clear expectations, providing continuous feedback, and recognizing achievements.
* **Feedback:** Should be specific, timely, and constructive.
* **Rewards:** Both intrinsic (recognition, personal growth) and extrinsic (pay, bonuses) rewards play a role in maintaining high performance.

## Supplementary: Project Management & Speed Networking

### Project Management Basics
Managing a team project effectively requires structure and planning.
* **Initiation:** Defining the project scope, goals, and team roles.
* **Planning:** Creating a timeline, assigning tasks, and identifying potential risks.
* **Execution:** Carrying out the work while maintaining communication and managing conflicts.
* **Monitoring/Controlling:** Tracking progress against the plan and making necessary adjustments.
* **Closing:** Reviewing the outcomes, documenting lessons learned, and celebrating success.

### Speed Networking for Group Work
Networking within groups rapidly accelerates trust and understanding.
* **Purpose:** Quickly learning about team members' skills, backgrounds, and working styles to assign roles more effectively.
* **Practice:** Engaging in structured, short conversations focusing on past experiences, strengths, and personal goals related to the module.
