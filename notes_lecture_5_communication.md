## What is Communication?

> Communication is the **process of creating shared understanding**.  
> Meaning is **interpreted, not transmitted**. It involves symbols (words, images, gestures) and requires feedback to check understanding.

**Why Communication Matters:**
- Shapes culture and leadership
- Influences performance and trust
- Builds (or destroys) relationships
- Reduces misunderstanding and conflict
- Enables effective decision-making
- Supports change implementation

---

## Why Study Communication? — Organisational Impact

| Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Coordination** | Improves task integration, reduces ambiguity and role conflict |
| **Leadership** | Builds credibility, shapes vision, enhances engagement |
| **Culture** | Transmits values/norms, reinforces identity and shared assumptions |
| **Change** | Reduces anxiety during restructuring, clarifies expectations |

## The Communication Process Model

### Sender & Encoding
- Idea originates from the **sender**
- **Encoding:** translating thoughts into symbols (words, tone, gestures)
- Influenced by: assumptions, emotions, status, cultural background
- ⚠️ **Intention ≠ Impact**

### Channel & Noise
- Channels include: email, face-to-face, meetings, text/chats, reports
- **Noise** disrupts clarity:
  - *Physical noise* — distractions in the environment
  - *Psychological noise* — bias, stress, assumptions

### Receiver & Feedback
- **Decoding** is shaped by personal perception and prior experience
- **Feedback** confirms, modifies, or distorts meaning
- Communication is **interactive, not linear**

---

## Interpersonal Communication

### Active Listening
- Give full attention (verbal and non-verbal signals)
- Reflect feelings and meanings back
- Reduces misunderstanding significantly

### Psychological Safety
- A climate where individuals **feel safe to speak up**
- Encourages openness; reduces defensive behaviour

### Perception & Attribution
- Messages are filtered through personal assumptions
- Stereotyping and bias distort interpretation
- Attribution errors affect workplace relationships

---

## Verbal Communication

| Aspect | Key Point |
|---|---|
| **Language Choice** | Framing affects perception; clarity improves understanding; avoid jargon |
| **Tone & Delivery** | Tone communicates attitude; inconsistency between words and tone reduces trust |
| **Framing & Sensemaking** | Leaders frame events to shape meaning; positive/negative framing influences morale |

**Barriers in Verbal Communication:**
- Ambiguity
- Euphemisms
- Over-complex language
- Cultural differences in interpretation

---

## Non-Verbal Communication

- **Body language:** posture signals confidence; gestures reinforce message
- **Paralanguage:** tone, pitch, speed — often **overrides** spoken words
- Non-verbal signals frequently convey the *true* feelings behind words

---

## Cultural Differences in Communication

| Dimension | Low-Context (e.g. UK, USA) | High-Context (e.g. Japan, Middle East) |
|---|---|---|
| Style | Explicit, direct, task-focused | Implicit, relationship-focused |
| Feedback | Direct and clear | Indirect and subtle |
| Communication | Clear written instructions | Context-dependent interpretation |

---

## Impression Management

> Conscious or unconscious attempts to **shape others' perceptions**.

**Common Strategies:**
- **Self-promotion** — highlighting competence and achievements
- **Ingratiation** — seeking approval through praise or agreement
- **Exemplification** — appearing dedicated or morally committed
- **Supplication** — highlighting limitations to gain support

**Ethical Considerations:**
- Risk of manipulation
- Authenticity matters
- Impact on trust and credibility

---

## Emotional Intelligence (EI)

> *"Emotional Intelligence is the ability to identify, integrate, understand and reflectively manage one's own and other people's feelings."*

**Five Dimensions:**

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| **Self-awareness** | Recognising your own emotions |
| **Self-regulation** | Managing emotional reactions |
| **Motivation** | Inner drive to achieve |
| **Empathy** | Sensitivity to others' emotional cues |
| **Social Skills** | Building relationships; managing conflict |

## Organisational Communication Flows

### Formal Flows
- **Downward** — instructions, policy, performance expectations
- **Upward** — feedback, suggestions, concerns

### Informal Flows
- **Grapevine** — spreads rapidly; shapes morale
- Can spread accurate *or* inaccurate information

| | **Open Climate** | **Defensive Climate** |
|---|---|---|

## Digital Communication

**Advantages:**
- Speed and accessibility
- Broader reach
- Asynchronous flexibility
- Easy documentation and traceability
- Supports remote and hybrid work

**Challenges:**
- Reduced richness (less non-verbal cues)
- Virtual fatigue
- Delayed or absent feedback
- Information overload

---

## Communication and Power

**Information Control:**
- Gatekeeping influence
- Agenda setting
- Selective disclosure of information
- Filtering upward communication

**Language & Identity:**
- Language reinforces hierarchy
- Labels shape organisational identity
- Narratives construct organisational reality
- Framing legitimises authority

---

## UK Case Insight: NHS Communication Breakdowns

**Problems identified:**
- **Upward silence** — employees didn't speak up; problems were known at lower levels but senior management was unaware
- **Departmental silos** — departments worked separately with no information flow between them

**Lessons learned:**
- **Transparency** — open sharing of information, honest communication
- **Psychological Safety** — people must feel safe to raise concerns without fear of punishment

---

## Summary

| Insight | Implication |
|---|---|
| Communication **constructs reality** | Leaders must be intentional with language and framing |
| Meaning depends on **context** | Same message can mean different things to different people |
| Strategic communication **drives effectiveness** | Design communication systems, not just send messages |
| Emotion and culture **shape outcomes** | EI and cultural awareness are leadership competencies |
