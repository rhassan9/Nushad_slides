## What is Team Building?

> *"Bringing a number of people together to perform a task does not necessarily mean they will work together effectively as a team — even when complementary skills, balanced personalities and a common goal are present."*

**Team Building** = *"Activities designed to improve interpersonal relationships and increase team cohesiveness."*

- Encourages informal communication — vital for building trust and good working relationships
- Enables a diverse group to work together effectively to achieve the project's goals
- Individuals naturally view a project through the lens of **their own discipline and background**
- The project manager must help members see the **big picture** and focus on overall goals together
- Obtaining **team spirit and commitment** is what team building is all about

> **Historical note:** In the late 1980s–1990s, many companies recognised team building as key to service quality and competitive advantage — yet the term often felt nebulous in practice.

---

## Why Team Building Must Be Accelerated

**Natural (unaided) team building:**
- Does occur as people work toward a common goal
- But for **short-duration projects**, natural development is too slow and too ad hoc to be valuable

**The project manager's role:**
- Use team-building techniques **as soon as the team is formed**
- The **fully operational team must be made to happen** — it doesn't self-assemble
- Accelerated team building raises the level of teamwork **earlier** in the project lifecycle

## The Four Levels of Team Building

| Level | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **1 – Interpersonal** | Members get to know each other, build trust, begin cohesion | Establish basic relationships |
| **2 – Team Roles** | Members identify preferred roles, understand expectations | Role clarity and fit |
| **3 – Shared Vision** | Team converges on one direction, one plan, one method | Alignment on goals and approach |
| **4 – Task Focused** | Team improves efficiency and productivity through practice | Project performance |

> *Relationships → Role Clarity → Shared Direction → Project Performance*

### Level 1: Interpersonal Team Building
The first level focuses on team members simply **getting to know each other** — under work pressure, this does not always happen naturally.

**Project manager's role:**
- Encourage members to talk to each other
- Help them learn one another's backgrounds, work experiences, hobbies, interests, and tastes
- Create situations where talking and listening can build interpersonal bonds and trust

**Why it matters:** Better understanding of personalities and behaviour improves communication and becomes the **first step toward team cohesion**.

## Outdoor Team Building

### Why Outdoor Activities?

**Traditional management training** (lectures, case studies) teaches theory and knowledge — but *fails to create a real-life dynamic situation*: there is no live interaction and no real risk.

**Outdoor courses add what is missing:**
Outdoor pursuits become the training vehicle for **real-life, interactive, problem-based learning** with immediate feedback on performance.

Management skills tested and developed outdoors:
- Interaction and teamwork skills
- Competitive skills
- Problem-solving skills
- Decision-making skills
- Communication skills
- Leadership skills
- Following / supporting skills

---

### Outdoor Action Learning: Education vs. Adventure

> For outdoor adventure to be *educational*, it must be part of an **integrated programme** with frequent opportunities for reflection.

The **Experiential Learning Cycle:**

| Step | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Carry out a task** | Participate in the outdoor activity |
| **Reflect** | Think about what happened |
| **Discuss** | Share observations with the team |
| **Analyse & Evaluate** | Assign your own meaning to the experience |

> *"People seldom learn from experience unless they assess it and assign their own meaning."*  
> *"People often learn more from mistakes than from successes."*

**Key distinction:**
- The **physical event** should be secondary
- The **primary activity** is learning about team building, leadership, communication, and problem solving
- Members must accept **responsibility and accountability** for instructions delegated to fellow team members

---

### Case Example: Sailing as Team Building

Sailing is used as the featured outdoor team-building vehicle because of its unique environment:

| Feature | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Isolation** | Once the yacht leaves the dock, the crew cannot simply walk away |
| **Apparent danger** | Heightens awareness; success or failure depends on the team alone |
| **Competition** | Racing against other crews drives interaction — failure lets the whole side down |
| **Coordination** | Manoeuvres like tacking succeed *only* when several people act simultaneously |
| **Immediate feedback** | Management theories can be tried and tested in real time |

**Activities include:** Tacking, sail changes, anchoring, man-over-board recovery, yacht racing

**On return:** Discussion sessions let participants review the day's exercises and relate management principles back to their actual workplace.

> *"Sailing offers 'learning-by-doing' with others who are also learning-by-doing — the only sure way of knowing that team members can work together effectively as a team."*
